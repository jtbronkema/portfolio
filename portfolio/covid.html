Developing a COVID-19 Strategy for the U.S. Government

Tableau | Data Cleaning, Data Visualization, Forecasting, Presenting

Project Completed by: Jackson Bronkema, Pavan Ghantasala, Sahil Patil, Mithull Singh

Problem Statement

In my data visualization class, we were tasked with researching COVID-19 data online and using our findings in the data to consult the United States Government on strategies they should take to decrease the spread of the virus. With our research and analysis complete, we then presented our recommended strategies in a 10-minute video presentation.

Our Analysis

Our data analysis consisted of four parts that worked together to bring a comprehensive solution to our client. First, we analyzed the global implications of the virus and how the United States has done with managing the spread of the virus in comparison to other countries around the world. Next, we did extensive research to confirm the efficacy of vaccines. Following this, we performed a variant analysis to understand how well vaccines have performed against different strains of the virus. Lastly, we looked at the economic implications of lockdowns, whether masks are truly effective, and how the virus is affecting Americans socially and psychologically.

Global Scenario

We wanted to look at how countries around the world were managing the COVID-19 pandemic, to better understand the areas that the United States was succeeding and some strategies that we could suggest for improvement. The metrics we looked at were the Stringency Index (how stringent the policies of each country were against COVID) and the number of hospital beds per 1,000 people. We wanted to see if there was a correlation between these metrics and the number of total cases and total deaths in a country. This research gave us a better perspective on how effectively different countries have managed the virus.

Vaccine Efficacy

During our research into the effectiveness of the vaccines released in the United States, we found that:

  1. The initial result of the release of the vaccine was a significant decrease in the number of new cases and deaths from COVID-19
  2. After the initial decrease in deaths and new cases, the number of cases has increased steadily in recent months
  3. People are not getting vaccinated at as high of a rate as they were; the growth rate of vaccination rate began to level off in May of 2021
  4. The growth rate of cases is increasing at a higher rate in previous months than the vaccination rate

We can garner that the vaccines were very effective in the early stages of their release, but just a few months later, they are no longer as effective. This can be attributed to low long-term efficacy, or to the arrival of new variants. In the next section, we took a deep dive into vaccine efficacy on different COVID-19 variants to understand why the vaccines are not as effective as they were in early 2021.

Variant Analysis

Through our research and data analysis, it became evident that COVID-19 variants grow and spread very quickly. In completing a variant analysis, we found:

  1. The Delta Variant only made up roughly 1% of the total cases in the U.S. on May 2, 2021. By August 1, 2021, 83.4% of the cases in the U.S. were the Delta Variant
  2. The Delta Variant is 2.37 times as deadly as the original strain
  3. The Delta Variant is 1.97 times as transmissible as the original strain
  4. For every person who is infected with the Delta Variant, we can expect them to infect 7 other people on average, making it more transmissible than HIV, SARS, and Ebola

We also conducted research to compare the effectiveness of the Johnson & Johnson, Moderna, and Pfizer vaccines. From this, we drew the conclusion that the Moderna and Pfizer vaccines are 11% more effective against the Delta Variant than Johnson & Johnson.

Lockdowns, and Social and Psychological Effects

Before giving our recommendations, we conducted a study on Nordic countries to compare the results of lockdowns and economic growth during the pandemic. We found that:

  1. Sweden had anywhere from three to eight times as many deaths as the other three Nordic countries. This gives evidence to the efficacy of lockdowns, since Sweden had few restrictions and Denmark, Finland, and Norway had much stronger restrictions.
  2. Despite not locking down, Sweden failed to outperform the other Nordic countries that did (by per capita GDP since March 2020)
  3. Common research also shows that wearing non-cloth masks reduces the spread of the virus by 65%

However, there seem to be some negative social and psychological effects of lockdowns and COVID-19 as a whole. As a result of the pandemic:

  • 45% of people in the U.S. say that they are experiencing increased financial stress
  • 41% of people say that they are more stressed about work
  • 60% of people say that they are more stressed than they were because of lockdowns

Recommendations

With our analysis conducted, our recommendations to the U.S. Government were as follows:

  1. Continue to keep a global perspective and research what is working and not working in other countries that are similar to the U.S.
  2. Continue to market the vaccines, as they are very effective against the original strain of the virus
  3. Market the Moderna and Pfizer vaccines over the Johnson & Johnson, since they are 11% more effective against the more dangerous Delta Variant
  4. Make masks mandatory indoors until the data shows that they are no longer a significant factor in stopping the spread of COVID-19
  5. Try to lock down for as short a period as possible. This slows the spread of the virus, but the social and psychological results of lockdowns are severe

Conclusion and Areas of Continued Research

This project was very challenging because there is a lot of stochasticity involved in trying to find solutions to a problem with so many different variables. We had the advantage of having a few months of data on the virus when making our recommendations to the U.S. Government. I feel that we have provided many powerful and data-driven solutions that should be implemented going forward. With a problem of this nature, there are also many other variables that we would have liked to investigate if we had more time. A few of these areas of further research include:

  1. Run a regression model using the global variables such as stringency, hospital beds per 1,000 people, and economic support to predict the number of cases or deaths and see which independent variables are statistically significant
  2. Research how the Delta Variant would have affected the U.S. if the majority of people did not have the vaccine prior to the emergence of Delta
  3. Get statistics on the effects of COVID by age group. This could give us an idea of how to handle the virus in schools, universities, nursing homes, etc.
  4. Research further the economic impact of government support in the long term, with a data-driven cost-benefit analysis
